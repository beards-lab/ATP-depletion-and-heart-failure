Transferring the low-ATP fit onto the realign4 8 mM baseline

Question. The 8 mM baseline moved on Further-optim (realign4_opt, the load-realignment refit). The low-ATP fit was built on the previous baseline (rskR2_w025_opt). Re-base it — new baseline, same ATP gates, no refit — and ask how good the 2 mM fit is now.

Verdict. The transfer works and it is a large, real improvement: 2 mM feature cost 19.58 → 12.44 on one fixed yardstick, against a scatter floor of ~0.25. Refitting the gates on the new baseline then buys a further 12.44 → 11.20 (§5).

But the gain is not the gates doing something new — it is that realign4 fixed a defect the two conditions share, and the 2 mM condition inherited it. The specifically-ATP part of the residual survives both steps intact: rsK's ratio is 0.587 against a measured 0.363, versus 0.600 before any of this. The refit, given 21 free ATP exponents, moved rsK by 0.02 and spent two of its three accepted moves on mechanical levers (eta_M, kSE) rather than rate constants. The kinetic gates are exhausted; what remains is structural.

1. What was transferred

The low-ATP specifics are a cleanly separable block — everything else in lowATP_powergate2_opt.m is the old 8 mM baseline. So the re-base is exactly: realign4_opt.m verbatim, plus

  • UseAtpPowerGates = 1, MgATP_ref = 8
  • the 21 pAtp_* exponents
  • the 2 mM condition itself (MgATP = 2, Pi = 0.8, 2 mM slack protocol)

written to params/lowATP_realign4_gates.m. The gates are exactly 1 at MgATP_ref for any exponents, so the 8 mM fit above them is untouched by construction.

Exponents were taken from the optimiser state at full precision, not from the committed %.6g snapshot — that rounding alone is worth ~0.60 of the 2 mM objective (AtpGateHillForm §3a).

Merging Further-optim was a prerequisite, not a convenience: realign4_opt sets UseLoadRealign, SRXFromR2HighStrain, UseMaxwellTensionOnly and the FV_normpowerAvg feature, none of which existed on lowatp-2state.

2. The numbers

The two baselines were fitted against different objectives, so the incumbent's recorded 15.98 is not comparable to anything below. realign4's list drops ovrsht_dy (retired by SlackDataAnalysis), reweights rsK 0.1 → 0.25, and rescales the passive terms. Everything here is therefore scored both ways, and the like-for-like column is the one to read.

configuration OLD list (19 feats) NEW list (18 feats)
OLD baseline + gates @ 2 mM 15.985 19.575
NEW baseline + gates @ 2 mM 15.687 12.440
NEW baseline, gates OFF @ 2 mM 133.49 138.30
OLD baseline @ 8 mM (floor) 4.685 5.669
NEW baseline @ 8 mM (floor) 6.738 3.112

Three readings, in order of importance:

  • On the current objective the 2 mM fit improves 19.58 → 12.44 (−36 %), ~29× the measured peak-to-peak scatter of 0.25. The 8 mM floor improves 5.67 → 3.11, so the excess over floor goes 13.91 → 9.33 (−33 %). Roughly a third of the win is the shared baseline getting better and two thirds is better low-ATP behaviour — though that split is a framing, not a decomposition, since the features interact.
  • On the old objective the same change is worth only −0.30 — about one peak-to-peak, i.e. nothing. The entire improvement lives in rsK, which the old list weighted at 0.1 and which realign4 was built to fix. The two lists disagree about whether this transfer is a triumph or a wash; the new one is right, because rsK is where the model is actually wrong.
  • The transferred gates still carry the whole low-ATP effect on the new baseline: turning them off costs 12.44 → 138.30. They did not become inert or harmful under the new parameters, which is the thing that could have gone wrong and did not.
Constant offset, read before quoting any absolute number. The bound term k2|…,ksrd|…,kmsrd|… contributes exactly 1.200 to every cell above. A slack-only run does not produce k2/ksrd/kmsrd, so each sub-entry returns MISSING_FEATURE_COST = 100 × its weight (0.01/0.001/0.001). It is not a bound violation and not a residual. It cancels in every comparison here, but the honest floors are 1.91 (new) and 4.47 (old), not 3.11 and 5.67.

4. The physiology: is the ATP effect any better?

Mostly no, and this is the honest limit of the result. Ratios are 2 mM / 8 mM:

rsK 0.363 0.600 0.588 1.62
  • Force amplitudes are excellent and were already: force, A and peak1_y all land within 1–2 % of the measured ATP effect. The 2 mM force enhancement is a solved problem in this model.
  • rsK's ratio barely moved (0.600 → 0.588, still 1.6× too shallow) — but its absolute value improved a lot at both concentrations: 8 mM 102.6 → 67.1 (data 43.7, so ×2.35 → ×1.53) and 2 mM 61.6 → 39.5 (data 15.9, ×3.88 → ×2.48). That is exactly the shape of a defect shared with 8 mM, which is what AtpGateHillForm §5.4 predicted would be immune to anything done to the gates. Fixing it at 8 mM fixed it at 2 mM. The residual ATP asymmetry in rsK is a separate, still-open defect.
  • ktr, vall_y and peak2 drifted slightly the wrong way (m/d 0.87–0.89): the model now over-slows ktr at low ATP and under-produces the low-ATP enhancement of the valley and second peak. These are the terms that got worse in §3 and they are the natural targets for a refit.

5. The refit

Round 3, RunGateRefit.m: the same 21 exponents, seeded from the transferred values, optimised on the realign4 baseline against its own feature list. Three changes from round 2, all forced by AtpGateHillForm §3a — accept threshold 0.25 (the measured peak-to-peak scatter) instead of 1e-4, coarse steps, and %.17g serialisation. Converged in 36.5 min / 79 evaluations, well inside the 3 h budget: it ran out of moves, not time.

2 mM cost 12.4399 → 11.2037 (−1.24, −10 %). Written to params/lowATP_realign4_gates_opt.m. 8 mM invariance asserted exactly zero.

Only three moves cleared the threshold, out of 21 gates:

gate tier seed → refit ×factor at 2 mM Δcost
kmsrd SRX-ADP return A +0.636 → +0.486 0.414 → 0.510 −0.251
eta_M Maxwell dashpot A* 0.000 → −0.200 1.000 → 1.319 −0.464
kSE series elastic B +0.070 → +0.150 0.907 → 0.812 −0.521

The other 18 — including every core rate constant: k2, kah, kamh, ka, kd, k1, k_1, ksr0, ksr2srd, ksrd2sr — did not move at all. Stage 1 tested the nine already-active gates at ±0.15/±0.30 and accepted exactly one.

5a. What the refit is telling us, which is more than the −1.24

The gains are in the mechanical levers, not the kinetic ones. eta_M (a viscosity) and kSE (a series stiffness) together supply 0.99 of the 1.24 — and both are the levers this project has flagged as the weakest physiological claims (getParams calls eta_M "least defensible"; kSE is tier B by construction). The cross-bridge rate gates are done: they were already at a local optimum, and the objective's remaining low-ATP degrees of freedom are compliance, not chemistry.

That is exactly what §4's conflict predicts. ktr needed less low-ATP suppression and rsK needed more, on two rates driven by the same core cycle, so no strain-uniform kinetic gate can serve both. The optimiser did the only thing available: it took the one kinetic move that helps ktr (kmsrd, the ktr lever, relaxed 0.414 → 0.510) and paid for the rest with series compliance.

The refit recovered the terms the transfer had broken — ktr, A, ktr_rmse and vall2_dy were four of the six that got worse in §3, and all four came back. ktr's ratio improves 0.469 → 0.504 against a measured 0.537 (m/d 0.87 → 0.94).

rsK did not move — 2.756 → 2.736. It is now 24 % of the whole 2 mM residual and by far the largest single term, and 21 free ATP exponents bought 0.02 of it. Its ratio sits at 0.587 against a measured 0.363 (m/d 1.61, unchanged). This is the cleanest statement yet that the residual low-ATP defect is not reachable by any 8 mM-inert rate gate: the levers exist, the optimiser has them, and they do nothing.

vall_y and peak2 also stay stuck at m/d 0.90 — the model under-produces the low-ATP enhancement of the valley and second peak, and no gate fixed that either.

5b. Two caveats on the number

  • kmsrd's move cleared the threshold by 0.001 (−0.251 against 0.25). At σ ≈ 0.076 that is ~3.3σ — defensible, but it is the one move here that a re-run might not reproduce. The eta_M and kSE moves are ~6σ and ~7σ and are not in question.
  • All costs still carry the constant +1.200 bound-term artifact (§2). The refit's honest numbers are 11.24 → 10.00 against a floor of 1.91.

Side note, in the model's favour: the refit moved kmsrd's 2 mM factor from 0.414 to 0.510, which raises its implied Km from 7.15 mM toward ~5 mM and its k/K from 2.8×10³ toward ~4×10³ M⁻¹s⁻¹. Still 100× too slow to be ATP binding, so AtpGateHillForm's falsification stands — but the fit moved toward chemical plausibility when given the freedom, not away from it.

6. What this does and does not license

  • Does: adopt params/lowATP_realign4_gates.m as the low-ATP snapshot. It is strictly better than the incumbent on the current objective and the gates transfer intact.
  • Does not: claim the low-ATP mechanism improved. No low-ATP parameter was refitted, and the ATP ratios are the same or marginally worse except through rsK's shared component.
  • The refit is now done (§5) and lands at 11.20. It recovered the terms the transfer broke, but only by spending series compliance, and it did not touch rsK.
  • Next is not another gate fit. Round 3 had 21 free exponents and used three, two of them mechanical. The kinetic gates are exhausted. rsK is 24 % of the residual and moved by 0.02, so the next step has to be structural: something that can slow the post-restretch recovery at low ATP without slowing ktr. UseStrainDepAtpGate (already wired, default-off) is the cheapest candidate — it makes the apparent Km(ATP) rise with strain, which is exactly the rsK-vs-ktr separation a strain-uniform gate cannot express. It has never been fitted.

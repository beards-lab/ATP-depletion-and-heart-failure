Which pathways does [ATP] act on? — the hypothesis ladder

Question. The 21-gate fit lets [ATP] modulate any rate or mechanical parameter, including ones with no nucleotide chemistry. That is the wildest hypothesis. How does it compare with progressively more constrained, physiologically defensible sets?

Answer. Three chemically-defensible parameters capture 97.8 % of the entire ATP effect; seven capture 99.5 %. The other fourteen buy 0.5 %, and they buy it with mechanics, not chemistry.

1. Setup

Core frozen at realign4 — only ATP-gate exponents move. Every gate is exactly 1 at MgATP_ref, so the 8 mM leg is invariant by construction; this was asserted for every rung at |Δ| = 0.000e+00, which is also what makes the experiment affordable (only the 2 mM leg needs scoring).

The 2 mM objective now includes force-velocity and power — see §5.

The 21 gateable knobs, classified by whether ATP should touch them:

class members rationale
CHEM k2, kah, kamh the nucleotide is a substrate of the step. k2 = ATP-induced actomyosin dissociation; kah/kamh = hydrolysis and its reverse
SRX kmsrd, ksr0, ksr2srd, ksrd2sr no ATP in the rate law, but the states differ in bound nucleotide — arguable
MECH kstiff2, kSE, kSE_M, mu_neg, dr, dr2, P_bound_max stiffness/viscosity/geometry. No nucleotide chemistry
OTHER ka, kd, k1, k_1, tau_reg, A0 kinetic but not ATP steps: actin attachment, power stroke, availability

Marginal gains, and how they compare with the measured noise floor (§3):

Two caveats, both against the conclusion I am drawing:

  1. H4 did not converge. 21 gates × 4 steps needs ~84 evaluations per sweep and its budget allowed one sweep. 13.79 is a floor, not an optimum. H1–H3 all converged (they stopped improving, not timed out).
  2. The accept threshold was too strict, not too loose. 0.25 against a measured peak-to-peak of 0.106 means every rung rejected real improvements. The bias is equal across rungs, so the ranking holds, but every absolute number is pessimistic.

Neither caveat rescues the wild hypothesis: it is given every benefit and still costs 14 extra parameters for 1.17.

3. The noise floor — I predicted this wrong

I expected the floor to rise when FV entered the objective (bigger cost, more terms). It fell, by a factor of 2.4:

objective σ peak-to-peak
2 mM slack only (AtpGateHillForm) 0.076 0.25
2 mM slack + FV (this one) 0.0362 0.106

Measured by nudging pAtp_kmsrd along ±1e-9 … ±1e-5 — deltas far too small to mean anything — and confirming the response is non-monotone in δ, i.e. scatter rather than sensitivity.

The reason is instructive: force-velocity is a set of steady isovelocity forces with no event-triggered feature extraction, so it adds a large smooth component that dilutes the rough slack-transient one. Adding data made the objective better-conditioned, not just bigger. Consequently all three marginal gains above are real — H3 and H4 genuinely are better than H2. They are just negligible in proportion, and they are not chemistry.

Three things worth reading off this table:

(a) The wild hypothesis's two finds are both non-chemical. kSE_M (Maxwell spring, −0.52) and tau_reg (registration-availability recovery, −0.32) are H4's entire advantage. Given free rein over 21 knobs, the fit does not discover a new ATP pathway — it bends mechanics. That is what a competing hypothesis looks like when it is flexible rather than right.

(b) Adding the 2 mM FV data flipped the SRX-entry gates. ksr0 and ksr2srd both go from ATP-suppressed (+0.10, +0.05) to ATP-enhanced (−0.12, −0.10), identically and reproducibly in H2, H3 and H4. Low ATP now drives heads into SRX — the physiologically expected direction (energy conservation / IHM), and the opposite of what the pre-FV fit said. The new data corrected a sign error in the mechanism.

(c) The exponents are not individually identifiable. kah refits to +0.250 in H1 and +0.030 in H2 — same step, same data, different companions. This has a consequence beyond this run: AtpGateHillForm converted these exponents into per-step Km values and tested them against solution chemistry, concluding that kmsrd's implied on-rate is 100–1000× too slow to be ATP binding. Those Km values are conditional on which gate set was free, not properties of the step, so that falsification is weaker than reported there.

5. The 2 mM force-velocity data, and a prediction of mine it kills

Data_ATP and Data_ATP_AB2021 both carry a 2 mM column (ATP_c = [8 4 2]). runFVExperiment already computed i_atp = 3 correctly and the least-squares path already used it — but the hardcoded features_data table scored every condition against the 8 mM curve. That table is the one I introduced when resolving the Further-optim merge, choosing transcribed constants over the version that indexed the loaded data. Now fixed: both source sets are read at the current [ATP], with a guard for AB2021's absent 4 mM arm. 8 mM cost moves 3.851300 → 3.851922 (Δ 0.0006, from the isometric normaliser being 56.4048 rather than the transcribed 56.40).

This falsifies a prediction I gave you two reports ago. I wrote that the model predicts peak power shifting from 1.0 → 0.5 ML/s at low ATP, called it "a cheap discriminating experiment", and said the series did not exist. It existed.

peak power at v
data, 8 mM 0.649 1.0 ML/s
data, 2 mM 0.469 1.0 ML/s — no shift
model, 2 mM 0.379 0.5 ML/s

The data show no leftward shift. The model's shift is a model artifact, and 2 mM FV is now a live residual: normalised force at v = 1 reads 0.512 in data vs 0.352 in model, 31 % low.

6. Figures — the H2 model

Honest reading of the feature grid: amplitudes stay excellent (steady 1 %/3 %, A 2 %/4 %, peak1 4 %/2 %), and rsK is still wrong — and slightly worse at 2 mM than in H3 (171 % vs 153 %), because the mechanical gates H2 drops were the ones slowing it. vall2_dy at 2 mM likewise degrades to 56 %. So H2 is the more defensible model, not a uniformly better-fitting one; it buys interpretability with ~0.32 of cost and some rsK.

7. What I take from this

  • Use H2 as the working low-ATP model. Seven parameters, every one attached to a step where a nucleotide plausibly acts, 99.5 % of the achievable fit, and it now gets the SRX-entry direction right.
  • The wild 21-gate hypothesis is not supported. It wins by 1.17 with 14 extra parameters, entirely via mechanics, and only as an unconverged floor.
  • rsK survives every version of this. Three fitting campaigns and four hypothesis classes; it is 60 % off at 8 mM in all of them. It is not an ATP-pathway question at all — it is an 8 mM structural defect that low ATP merely amplifies.
  • The most valuable thing here was adding data, not parameters. 2 mM FV cost nothing to include, tightened the objective's noise floor by 2.4×, flipped a mechanism sign to the physiologically correct direction, and killed a model prediction. That is a better return than any of the refits.

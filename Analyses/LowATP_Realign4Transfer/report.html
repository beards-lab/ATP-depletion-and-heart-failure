Low-ATP fit on the realign4 baseline — figure report

Parameters. 8 mM = params/realign4_opt.m (the Further-optim load-realignment best). 2 mM = params/lowATP_realign4_gates_opt.m — that same baseline plus the 21 ATP power gates, refitted here. The gates are exactly 1 at 8 mM by construction, so the two conditions share one parameter set; every 2 mM curve below is produced by changing [MgATP] alone.

Headline. 2 mM feature cost 19.58 → 12.44 → 11.20 (old baseline → re-based → refitted), against an 8 mM floor of 3.11 on the same 18-term list and a measured noise floor of 0.25. All costs include a constant +1.20 artifact (§5).

The force-velocity fit is the weakest part of the 8 mM model, and its error is structured, not random. The curve is pinned at both ends — exact at v = 0 by definition, −6.6 % at 4 ML/s — and sags through the middle, reaching −20.6 % at 2 ML/s. The model is too concave: it drops away from isometric too slowly (+6 % at 0.5) and then too fast (−10 %, −21 %). In power terms it under-produces peak power by 7.7 % and mid-range power by 16 %, well outside the ±0.02–0.05 spread between the two source datasets.

That shape is a curvature error, so the lever is the strain dependence of detachment, not a scalar rate. It is worth stating plainly that this is a fitted quantity that the current objective already weights heavily (FV_normpowerAvg, weight 62.3), and it still lands here.

Two testable 2 mM predictions, neither constrained by any data in the fit:

  1. Peak power falls to ×0.635 of its 8 mM value, while isometric force rises ×1.187. Low ATP is stronger and much less powerful.
  2. The optimal shortening velocity shifts down, from 1.0 to 0.5 ML/s. The power curve does not merely scale — it moves left, because detachment is the step being slowed.

Prediction 2 is the more useful one: it is a shape change, so it survives the absolute force-scale uncertainty that makes cross-prep comparisons hard, and a 2 mM isovelocity series over 0.25–4 ML/s would settle it. That series does not exist yet.

2. The whole slack protocol

Five shorten/re-stretch cycles at descending sarcomere length. Grey is data, colour is model; the lower trace of each pair is the commanded SL.

At this zoom the fit is good at both concentrations — the model reproduces the isometric level, the slack drop to zero force, the redevelopment, the re-stretch spike and the descending-limb trend as SL steps 2.04 → 1.88 µm. The 2 mM condition is visibly the stronger one (~95 vs ~80 kPa) and visibly the slower one, which is the ATP effect the whole project is about.

Two defects are already visible here and get quantified below: the 2 mM redevelopment lags the data through the middle of each rise (the red curve sits below grey from ~74.46 to ~74.62 s), and the model's re-stretch spike overshoots at 2 mM.

rsK model 63 s⁻¹ 37 s⁻¹ 0.587

This panel contains the project's open problem, and it is visible by eye. Look at the post-re-stretch recovery — the slow climb after peak2, right of 74.8 s:

  • At 8 mM the model recovers at 63 s⁻¹ against a measured 46 — ×1.4 too fast.
  • At 2 mM the model recovers at 37 s⁻¹ against a measured 15 — ×2.5 too fast.

The data slow this recovery down by ×0.36 when ATP drops; the model only manages ×0.59. k_tr in the same trace is nearly right (×0.504 vs ×0.537), so this is not a global kinetic scaling error — the model gets the isometric redevelopment rate right and the post-re-stretch one wrong, in the same segment, from the same parameters.

Also visible: the model's peak1 overshoots at 2 mM (~122 vs ~117 kPa) and its subsequent valley digs ~8 kPa too deep before recovering — the vall_y and vall2_dy residuals in §4.

Read the grid by colour: blue pairs (8 mM) mostly overlie each other; red pairs (2 mM) separate. The amplitude features are solved and the rate features are not. Force, amplitude and both peaks track the data across all five segments and both concentrations to within a few percent, including the drop at segment 5 where SL reaches 1.88 µm. That is the filament-overlap and stiffness machinery working.

rsK is the outlier by an order of magnitude — the only feature where the model and data curves do not even overlap, at either concentration, in any segment. It is 24 % of the total 2 mM residual on its own.

vall_t at 107 % looks alarming and mostly is not: the valley time is ~8–17 ms, so a 5 ms error reads as 60 %. It carries weight 0.2 for exactly that reason.

5. What the numbers mean, and one caveat

Cost, on one fixed 18-term list:

configuration 2 mM cost 8 mM floor
old baseline (rskR2) + gates 19.58 5.67
re-based onto realign4, gates unchanged 12.44 3.11
+ gates refitted here 11.20 3.11
realign4 at 2 mM with gates off 138.30 —

The caveat, before anything is quoted from this page. Every cost above carries a constant +1.200 that is not a fit residual: the composite bound term k2|…,ksrd|…,kmsrd|… returns MISSING_FEATURE_COST = 100 per sub-entry because a slack-only run does not produce those three features. It is identical in every configuration, so it cancels in every comparison here, but the honest numbers are 10.00 against a floor of 1.91.

What the refit did. Only 3 of 21 gates moved: kSE (−0.52), eta_M (−0.46) and kmsrd (−0.25). Two of the three are mechanical levers, and every core rate constant — k2, kah, kamh, ka, kd, k1, k_1, ksr0, ksr2srd, ksrd2sr — held still. It recovered k_tr, A and vall2_dy, which the re-base had cost, and moved rsK by 0.02.

The conclusion this report supports. Twenty-one free ATP exponents, converged, and rsK did not move. The obstruction is now specific: k_tr needs less low-ATP suppression while rsK needs more, and both are driven by the same core cycle, so no strain-uniform ATP gate can serve both — which is what §3 shows in one trace. The next lever has to be structural. UseStrainDepAtpGate (wired, default-off, never fitted) makes the apparent Km(ATP) rise with strain, which is exactly that separation.

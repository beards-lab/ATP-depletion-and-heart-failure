## 2. The pooling method, and how it differs from 03/27

### What the old target was

One preparation (`protocol_03_27_2026_8mM_slack.mat`), in that fibre's raw kPa,
**with no error bar anywhere**. Every residual was therefore unfalsifiable: a
15 % miss and a 2 % miss looked the same to the objective, because nothing said
how big 15 % is.

### The nuisance being removed

Tension is force ÷ *estimated* cross-sectional area. Across three preparations the
pre-slack isometric at SL 2.2 µm reads **79.8 / 70.0 / 50.9 kPa** — CV **22 %**.
Almost none of that is biology; it is the area estimate.

### The four steps

1. **Scale** — divide every force-dimension feature of preparation *p* by that
   preparation's own `Fss22`. **One divisor per preparation, shared by both ATP
   conditions**, so the 2 mM/8 mM ratio stays a *measurement* rather than a
   definition.
2. **Rundown** — lift the run recorded *second* in each session by
   `1 + φ·|slope|·T/F` with **φ = 0.452**, on *effective activated time*. Without
   it the ATP effect depends on running order, which differs across the three days
   by design.
3. **Pool** — mean of the per-preparation **features**, never features of an
   averaged trace.
4. **Spread** — *s* = between-preparation relative SD, **variance-moderated**
   toward the median of its class (Smyth 2004), prior d₀ = 2.58, giving 4.58 dof.

### Why one common scale is legitimate — and it is tested, not assumed

- **PC1 holds 88.3 %** of the between-preparation log-variance and its loading is
  flat (|cos| with all-ones = **0.955**). A pure common scale is exactly a rank-1
  matrix with a flat loading. Both hold.
- **Scale-free features are the control group.** `ktr`, `t0`, `rsK`, `rsR2` cannot
  be touched by any normalisation; their 11–38 % spread is the floor. After
  normalisation the force features sit at **4–11 %** — better than the best rate.

**Result: spread 29 % → 15 %.** Absolute tension goes from the worst-determined
quantity in the experiment to among the best.

### Two things the pooling settled

- **Which anchor barely matters.** A paired bootstrap (4000 draws) cannot separate
  the top three: P(best) 64 / 18 / 15 %. *Normalising vs not* is decisive;
  *which divisor* is not a decision n = 3 can support.
- **Pool features, never traces.** The notorious −24 % bias on `rsK` was not
  smearing — it was **one segment** whose 844 ms feature window ran past the
  570 ms averaging grid.

### The one place fitting 03/27 was actively misleading

> The pool's **first re-stretch peak sits 3 % below** its own isometric.
> **03/27's sits 12 % above.**

Fitting 03/27 alone was *rewarding* the model's lengthening overshoot — a known
artefact. The target moved by more than the model's own residual, so the switch
was not cosmetic.

And 03/27 is not otherwise a bad preparation: it sits at χ² = 0.39 against the
pooled mean where 0.67 is what three preparations scattering around their own mean
must give. **Pooling adds error bars, not a different target** — except in that
one feature, which is the one that mattered.

## 3. χ² weighting and the deadband floor

### Step 1 — make every term a χ²

```
w = λ · R / s²
```

`s` = measured between-preparation spread; `R` = ATP-relevance bonus; λ = one
global constant for cost-neutrality. Because the cost sums **squared relative**
residuals, dividing by `s²` makes every term a χ²: **1.0 = one preparation-width
from the data.** Terms become comparable, which they were not when weights
0.2 … 50 were set by hand.

### Step 2 — the failure that weighting alone could not fix

An objective is a fixed budget, and a search spends it where descent is cheapest —
which is reliably whichever term is *already best determined*, because it is the
smoothest.

> **The `rigaware` campaign:** F–V held **40 % of the objective at χ² = 0.10** — a
> tenth of one preparation-width — and absorbed **110 % of a 14-hour gain** while
> every feature the campaign existed to fix went backwards. Strip F–V and the fit
> was 1.8 % *worse*.

Re-pinning the weight cannot fix this, **and it was tried**: `FV_SHARE` 0.70 → 0.40
is what produced that run. Any nonzero weight on a smooth, nearly-solved term is
still the cheapest descent available.

### Step 3 — the floor

```matlab
c = max(0, c - floor_raw);          % deadband, not deletion
c = c + sum(isnan(fs)) * NAN_COST;  % NaN penalty applied AFTER
```

with

```
fl = FLOOR_CHI2 · nEl · s²        FLOOR_CHI2 = 1.0
```

**One number was chosen** (`FLOOR_CHI2 = 1.0`); every per-term value is derived.
Worked example — `steady`: s = 0.157604, nEl = 5 → 1.0 × 5 × 0.157604² =
**0.124196**, exactly the `fl:0.124196` in the seed.

It reads as: *being within one between-preparation SD of the pooled mean is free.*
A floored term still **defends itself** — it starts paying the moment it drifts out.

**Effect: 2.4088 → 0.9081.** 62 % of the objective was residual inside one
preparation-width. Composition flipped: F–V **40 % → 0 %**, re-stretch block
**28 % → 48 %**.

### The honest caveat — measured, not asserted

| snapshot | scored | raw | % of deadband | worst point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| seed | 0.000000 | 0.000535 | **1.0 %** | 0.48 sd |
| best | 0.000000 | 0.008819 | **17.1 %** | **2.06 sd** |

**The floor stops a solved term being *bought*. It does not stop it being *sold*.**
F–V's raw residual grew 16× and its worst point went 0.5 → 2.1 sd while the scored
cost stayed at exactly zero.

Why the band is that wide: `s` = 11.3 % there is the disagreement between two
**labs** — the loosest uncertainty F–V has — while the per-point weights use the
between-**curve** variance, which is far tighter. The term-level floor took the
generous notion. Fix is a per-term `FLOOR_CHI2` (F–V ≈ 0.25) or per-element floors.
**Not applied**: it changes the objective, and this campaign's result should stand
as measured.

## 4. How well do we fit 8 mM? The good, the bad, the ugly

### Good — at or inside the data's own resolution

| feature | residual (sd) |
|---|---|
| `Am`, `peak2`, `A`, `steady`, `rsA` | 0.03 – 0.15 |
| `vall2_dy`, `t0`, `vall2_t`, `vall_y`, `rsK` | 0.18 – 0.51 |
| `peak1_y` | 0.58 |
| `ktr` | 0.82 |
| `rsK80` | 0.98 |

Plus two structural wins:

- **The force LEVEL finally moved.** 71.5 kPa vs pooled 69.3 (+3 %, z = 0.20).
  Every previous generation sat at +15 % (z = 0.94), i.e. carrying 03/27's own
  scale. This had resisted several campaigns.
- **Timing came inside 2 sd.** `peak1_t`, `vall_t`, `peak1_dSL` were all beyond
  2 sd on the previous core; all three are now 1.37–1.63.

### Bad — coherent, not random

`peak1_t` 1.63 · `vall_t` 1.50 · `peak1_dSL` 1.37 · `rsT20` 1.35 ·
`restretchSlopeStart` 1.03

These are **four readings of one thing**: the shape and timing of the re-stretch
transient. They move together, which is why a 10-parameter draw struggles — the
direction that fixes them is higher-dimensional than the draw.

### Ugly — the three that are genuinely wrong

1. **`rsT0` at 3.14 sd** — the only feature beyond 2 sd, and it is **unscored**
   (not in `fn`), so this is an honest out-of-sample miss.
2. **The `rsK80` per-cycle SHAPE is wrong.** The model climbs monotonically
   across cycles (**51 → 64 s⁻¹**) where the data is flat at ~47 and then steps
   to 54 at cycle 5. Putting cycle 5 back improved the *level* (χ² 1.4 → 1.15)
   but bought **none of the shape**.
3. **The recovery is too fast in segments 2–4.** The model plateaus by ~360 ms
   while the data is still climbing at 420 ms. Visible directly on the timecourse.

Plus the F–V drift above, which the objective cannot see.

### Force–velocity

P/P₀ tracks well; normalised power — the quantity actually scored — sits below
the data above 1 ML/s: **0.605 vs 0.649** at 1 ML/s, **0.366 vs 0.413** at 4 ML/s.
Worth taking seriously because `DataPooling` §6 found the in-preparation FV2 ramp
lands **0.21 sd** from this borrowed curve at 8 mM, so the target is trustworthy
at this concentration.

## 5. Is there room for improvement, and is it realistic?

### The case that we are near the limit

- **`MechanismAblation`: 35 mechanisms flipped one at a time, 39.6 h, and
  *nothing* — added or removed — beats the incumbent beyond the noise.** The
  threshold is the campaign's own: two controls re-optimised to 0.9081 exactly,
  and the snapshot guard caught two phantom gains of 0.052 and 0.055, giving
  σ = 0.055.
- **An 11-DOF identifiability ceiling** from the Slack/FV/Ktr feature set. Adding
  parameters does not add constraint.
- **14 of 21 features are inside one preparation-width** — i.e. inside the data's
  own resolution. You cannot fit below the noise.
- Six of the model's parameters (the lattice block) carry only **two**
  identifiable combinations; four more were measured **inert**.

### The case that room remains

- The residual is **structured, not random**: one coherent defect (the re-stretch
  transient) across five features, plus a sign error in the `rsK80` cycle shape.
  Structure means mechanism, and mechanism is fixable.
- **Two protocols are not scored at all** — the staircase (`RunStairs = 0`) and
  ktr (`RunKtr = 0`). These are free out-of-sample tests, and the staircase is
  known to be wrong in an interesting way: elastic peak **2–4× too large**,
  phase-2 undershoot of the **wrong sign**, delayed recovery **absent**.
- **Two objective terms are unprincipled** — `PS_rampupRMSE` and
  `PS_holdDecayRMSE` are raw RMSEs with no measured spread, so no floor can be
  computed. They are **~18 % of the objective** and are the remaining exploitable
  smooth terms.
- **Searches stall rather than converge.** `estiff` moved 0.09 % in four rounds
  and never again, sitting on a flat ridge between 60×-tall walls — its
  "optimum" was where the search stopped. In this campaign profile *a* plateaued
  at round 6 while profile *c* was **still descending at round 13**.

### Verdict

> **The objective is close to its floor. The model has one structural defect
> left.** These are different statements, and conflating them is how a project
> concludes it is finished.

More optimiser hours on the current objective are **not** the lever — that is the
ablation's finding, measured over 39.6 h. What would move things:

1. **More preparations.** At n = 3 each weight is known only to a factor of ~18,
   so the objective's own shape is uncertain. 95–100 % of the spread in
   `A`/`ktr`/`t0`/`rsK` is biological, not extraction scatter — so more fibres,
   not better extraction.
2. **Score the held-out protocols**, staircase first. Features, not the trace:
   per-sample σ = 0.025–0.033 F₀ puts an irreducible floor of 0.006–0.011 on the
   MSE, and ~90 % of its weight sits in the holds.
3. **Fix the two unfloored terms** and the F–V floor width.
4. **A third [ATP]** — see §7.

## 6. The 2 mM side

### The phenotype, measured not estimated

| quantity | 2 mM / 8 mM |
|---|---|
| isometric force | **×1.36** |
| `ktr` (from the ktr protocol, CV 4 %) | **×0.55** |
| `rsK` | **×0.345** |
| force × `ktr` | ×0.74 |

Low ATP makes cardiac muscle **stronger and slower**. Consistent across four
preparations and both ATP running orders once rundown is removed.

**The structural result matters more than the numbers:** once each preparation is
scaled by its own steady force, *most features show no ATP effect at all* — `A`,
`peak1_y`, `vall_y`, `peak2`, `vall2_dy` all sit at 1.0 ± 0.1. **The 2 mM
phenotype is a force scale-up plus two rate slowdowns, not a change of waveform
shape** — and the model reproduces that structure.

### The gate ladder

Every ATP effect is a multiplicative gate on a rate, normalised to 1 at 8 mM, so
**the high-ATP fit is invariant by construction** — asserted at |Δ| = 0 before any
fitting. 21 rates are gateable.

| rung | gates | DOF | 2 mM cost | % of span |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H0 | none | 0 | 385.13 | 0 % |
| **H1** | `k2, kah, kamh` | 3 | **8.20** | **99.5 %** |
| H2 | + 4 SRX gates | 7 | 8.20 | 99.5 % |
| H3 | + mechanics | 10 | 7.64 | 99.7 % |
| H4 | all 21 | 21 | 6.43 | 100 % |

**Two gates do essentially everything** (`k2` 0.440, `kah` 0.300). The four SRX
gates buy *exactly* nothing. Everything beyond is compliance-bending, not pathway
discovery.

### But H1's chemistry is not defensible

`k2` is the canonical ATP step — ADP release + **ATP binding** + dissociation, the
only rate where free [ATP] enters the law directly. `kah`/`kamh` are hydrolysis of
**already-bound** ATP: free [ATP] does not appear. So the best-fitting rung is
fitted, not mechanistic.

Ranking biologically defensible sets on the old core:

| rank | id | gates | DOF | cost | tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **P1** | `k2, k_1` | 2 | **7.61** | C (Pi) |
| 4 | H1 | `k2, kah, kamh` | 3 | 8.20 | X |
| 5 | **C4** | `k2, ksrd2sr, kmsrd` | 3 | **8.26** | AB |
| 11 | C0 | `k2` | 1 | 11.62 | A |

**P1 wins on cost at the fewest DOF** — but note what it means: `k_1` is the
reverse stroke driven by **Pi rebinding**, i.e. P1 says the 2 mM solution differs
in Pi as well as ATP. Pi predicts *lower* force; the data show force ×1.34
*higher*. So if Pi contamination is present it is being over-compensated by
something else. **P1 fits best and explains least.**

### Two hard limits

- **Km is not identifiable.** The power law is the K→∞ limit of a Hill gate, and
  with data at only two [ATP] a gate is *one number* (its 2 mM factor) — so Km
  follows in closed form and **no refit is possible, in principle**. The two forms
  diverge ×0.4 on `ktr`/`rsK` by 0.25 mM and ×0.8 at 20 mM ⇒ **a third [ATP]
  discriminates them, and 20 mM is enough.**
- **One gate is falsified on its own physiology.** `kmsrd` — the largest ATP
  effect in the H-series fit — implies an ATP on-rate **100–1000× too slow to be
  ATP binding**. `kah`/`ksr0`/`ksr2srd` are textbook.

### What the 2 mM fit still fails

`rsK` (model 0.627 vs data 0.345 as a ratio; +8.8 sd absolute) and `vall2_dy`.
**`rsK` is not an ATP-pathway question** — no rung of the ladder moves it, and it
is 60 % off at 8 mM too. It is the same structural defect as §4's "ugly".

### The one-line answer to "is this the best we can do?"

**On this objective and this dataset, close to it.** The remaining gain is not in
the optimiser — 39.6 h of ablation says so. It is in *more data* (preparations, a
third [ATP], scoring the held-out protocols) and in *one mechanism* (the
re-stretch transient). Both are tractable; neither is another fitting campaign.
